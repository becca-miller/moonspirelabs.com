### How It Works

**1. Discovery call** (free)
We talk through what you're building, what stage you're at, and what success looks like. I'll let you know honestly whether I'm the right fit—and if I'm not, I'll try to point you in a better direction. At the end of this call, I'll give you a rough sense of timeline and budget range so you know what to expect.

**2. Discovery & scoping** ($3,000)
This is paid, professional scoping work that includes:
- Technical assessment of any existing code or prototypes
- Identification of risks, unknowns, and key decision points
- 2-3 scoping options with clear tradeoffs
- Fixed-price proposal for each path, with specific pricing based on the effort required

You'll receive a detailed proposal you can use to make decisions—whether you proceed with me or not. The pricing ranges listed above serve as general project size indicators; your proposal will include exact pricing tailored to your specific needs.

*If you move forward:* The discovery fee is fully credited toward your project cost if you start within 60 days.

**3. Build**
Fixed-price engagement based on the proposal. Regular check-ins, working software throughout, no hourly billing. Typical turnaround depends on project scope.

**4. Handoff**
Complete documentation, deployment, knowledge transfer, and 30 days of included support as you start using the system in production.

### My Approach

Every project starts with clarity. Before writing a single line of code, I work with you to understand what you're actually building, what constraints you're working within, and what success looks like.

I don't believe in "move fast and break things." I believe in making intentional tradeoffs—building systems that work today and can grow tomorrow, without accumulating technical debt that will slow you down later.

**What this means in practice:**
- I prioritize maintainability from day one
- I plan for extensibility without over-engineering for every possible future
- I help you distinguish between "this must be done right" and "this can evolve later"
- I surface risks and unknowns early, not after we've committed to an approach

Every engagement is scoped with clear deliverables, realistic timelines, and transparent pricing. No hourly billing, no scope creep, no surprises.

### Starling

**A music application for people who want to actually know their library.**

Streaming services give us infinite music but no connection to it. Starling is designed to help you listen more intentionally—to understand your own taste, curate your library, and develop a deeper relationship with the music you love.

**How it works:**
- Daily and ad-hoc listening sessions where you review tracks from your library
- Tag songs based on mood, themes, listening context, and what you actually like about them
- Build a personal understanding of your collection over time
- (Power users: customize your own tagging systems and sorting)

Starling isn't about algorithmic recommendations or endless discovery. It's about engaging with the music you already have—intentionally, thoughtfully, and on your own terms.

**Status:** Currently in active development. Beta testing planned for 2026.

### Background

I've spent years working across consulting, product development, and technical leadership roles—supporting small teams as they design, build, and maintain real-world software systems.

Before consulting full-time, I worked as a research data analyst in the anesthesiology department of a children's hospital, where I published studies and learned firsthand how research teams work—and how much they need software that respects the integrity of their work without getting in the way.

I have a background in biomedical engineering, and I've worked across industries from healthcare to media to AI/ML. What draws me to a project isn't the domain—it's the challenge of understanding new constraints, new problems, and new ways of thinking about software.

I care about software that makes the world better—even in small ways. That might mean helping a startup validate an idea that improves people's lives. It might mean turning a researcher's fragile prototype into something others can actually use. It might mean building an internal tool that saves a small team hours of manual work every week.

I don't believe in "move fast and break things." I think good software requires care, attention, and intentional tradeoffs. Sometimes things take longer to do properly, and I think that's time well spent.
